# Action Economy

## Actions Per Turn
You get a flat **2 actions**, every round, regardless of your Speed.
**Free movement** is baseline and revocable, hostile abilities or difficult terrain can strip it forcing movement to come out of your 2-action budget instead.

## Reactions & Tandem Actions
**Reactions** are an Action that takes less mental acuity or physical strain than a regular Action. Every character has a single free Reaction to use each round of play.

**Tandem Actions** spend a reaction. An acting character pays a normal action for their turn; though another player may assist by contributing something from their legacy to the Acting characters Action. To do so, the assisting character pays their Reaction. This assist can resolve as a certain effect from the secondary character being added to the Primary actor, or alternatively the assisting character can roll a pool of die based on practitioner of fitting scenario to replace the actor's lowest die in their 3-die pool roll if it's higher.

Whether you have a single or multiple reactions available this round depends on clearing Speed threshold ([[5. Speed & Turn Order]]). If your Speed sits above the round's average, you get **+1 additional reaction or tandem action** on top of your baseline reaction or tandem action.

>*Ren Talbot swings and rolls a 2, a 3, and a 1 — a 6. Yuki Marrow, reacting alongside him, rolls her lead Verve d6 and gets a 5. Since 5 beats Ren's lowest die (the 1), it swaps in: the roll becomes 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 instead of 6, still a clean 3-die sum, just with one die borrowed from an ally.*

Being fast buys you more chances to intervene in others' turns, not a bigger turn of your own, the faster you are, the more impact you can have on a fight beyond just your own actions.

## Interaction With Dice-Stepping and Pips

Stepping a die is purely a Pip cost. It does not consume an action, does not unlock an overflow action, and has no bearing on whether you clear the reaction threshold. The two economies, Pips and actions, run entirely independent of one another.
