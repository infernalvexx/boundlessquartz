# Turn Order

Turn order runs on **Speed** — a single, standing number on your character sheet, always known, never rolled for on the fly.

## Speed Is a Fact, Not a Roll

Outside combat, your GM can use your Speed as a passive lever for exploration and social scenes without you rolling it. A die roll only enters the picture once per encounter — at the top of combat, to set that fight's order.

**Baseline Speed** comes from three inputs at character creation: your Level (Ch2 §6), your bonded element's Reality Behavior (a fast-reading element sets a higher baseline than a slow, inert one — Ch4), and your Practitioner Type (Ch2 §2) — Casters and Wielders lean their respective Pool, while Shapers carry the lowest baseline of the three in exchange for extra flexibility below.

**This formula isn't replaced as you level — it's added to.** Both the shared Level-Up Baseline (Ch2 §1) and your Practitioner Type's own table (Ch2 §2) contribute a further Speed bonus at each level, stacking on top of the Level term above rather than overriding it.

## The Encounter-Start Roll

When combat begins, roll a Speed check: the Pool rolled is set by Practitioner Type (Casters roll Verve, Wielders roll Might, Shapers choose either), add your Baseline Speed, then add the tally of +/neutral/− tags carried by each Skill (Ch3 §2) assigned to whichever Pool you rolled. This sets your position for the round and does not recur mid-fight.

## Round Structure

Combat runs on a single turn queue — one character acts at a time, in Speed order, high to low. A round ends once everyone has acted. The queue locks at the top of the round: anything you do this turn can only affect where you sit in *next* round's queue, never this one.

## Ties

Tied characters each roll their highest Core Resource Pool (a normal 3-die sum, Ch1 §1). Higher roll wins and acts first — no new stat needed.

## The Speed Swing — What an Action Costs You Next Round

Every action feeds two numbers into your Speed for the following round, added directly together: the value carried by its Ability Type (Ch3 §1), and the value carried by the Approach (Ch3 §3) you chose to color it with.

| Type | Speed Value |
|---|---|
| Harmony | 4 |
| Sense | 3 |
| Burst | 2 |
| Creation | 1 |

> **Per action: Speed swing = Ability Type value + Approach value.**

*A Burst (2) delivered with Nerve (4) gives Kess a swing of 6 for next round. The same Burst delivered with Calculation (1) gives only 3 — the strike lands identically either way, but how Kess chose to make it costs real tempo.*

Because you take **2 actions per turn** (Ch3 §5), this happens twice, and both totals stack into your next-round Speed — turn order is meant to keep shifting as a direct consequence of what you chose to do, not settle into a static line-up.

## Speed-for-Action Trade

If your Speed sits 10 or more above the highest Speed in the round, you may spend 10 Speed to take an extra action that turn. The 10 spent feeds directly into your next-round Speed swing above, rather than being tracked separately.
