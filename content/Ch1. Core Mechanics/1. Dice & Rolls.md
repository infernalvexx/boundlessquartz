# Dice & Rolls

Every check you make in Project: Boundless resolves the same way. Learn this once, and you know how to play the whole game.

## The Roll

**Roll three dice, add them together.** You are not counting successes, and you're not hunting for one big number - you're summing three dice drawn from whichever Resource Pool (Blood, Might, or Verve - Ch2 §3) applies to what you're doing.

Each of your three dice sits in its own die-slot, and each slot has an independent size: **d4 → d6 → d8 → d10**. Because each slot grows on its own, a single Pool can carry mismatched dice - you might roll a d10, a d6, and a d4 together in the same Pool, depending on how your character has grown. This is deliberate: your capability broadens unevenly as you improve, rather than every roll simply getting a flat bonus.

## Pool Level & Expected Roll

Add up the tier of your three dice in a Pool - a d4 counts as tier 0, up through a d10 at tier 3 - and you get that Pool's **Pool Level**, running 0 through 9.

> **Expected Roll = 7.5 + Pool Level**

This gives you, and your GM, a quick way to gauge what a roll is likely to produce before the dice hit the table.

*Kess, a few weeks into adventuring, has a Might Pool of three plain d4s - Pool Level 0, an Expected Roll around 7.5. Rolling a 2, a 3, and a 1 gives a 6: a touch below expected. That's normal - Expected Roll is an average, not a guarantee.*

## Cross-Pool Checks

If the fiction supports it, you can pull dice from more than one Pool for a single check - a desperate shove that's part Might, part Verve-fueled adrenaline, for instance. Talk to your GM when a situation genuinely straddles two Pools.

## Why Sum, Not Count

A "count successes" system asks whether you're good or bad at a thing. Summing three growing dice asks something richer: as you improve, does your whole range of outcomes shift upward? Someone rolling three d4s and someone rolling three d10s aren't just "worse" and "better" versions of each other - their entire spread of possible outcomes has moved.
