# Skills

Nine Skills form the backbone of what your character is competent at:

| Skills    |          |            |
| --------- | -------- | ---------- |
| Fortitude | Instinct | Attunement |
| Grit      | Force    | Insight    |
| Focus     | Charisma | Craft      |

## How Skills Work

At character creation, you assign each of the nine Skills to one of the three Resource Pools: Blood, Might, or Verve. This assignment is permanent and it decides which Pool you roll whenever you use that Skill. The assignment itself grants no numeric bonus, it only decides which dice you reach for.

Skills answer "what are you doing" the raw category of action, independent of style.

>*Ren Talbot assigned Grit to Blood, so every time he leans on Grit: bracing against a blow, holding a door shut against a battering ram, he rolls his Blood Pool. Assign it to Might instead, and the same Skill draws on Might dice.*

## Why Skills Stay Fixed

Skills are your character's way of showcasing personality from day 0. Unlike your Legacy or Identity, which can grow and shift, your Skills and which Pool each rolls never change once set at creation. Two characters can have wildly different Legacies and still share every Skill-to-Pool assignment; two characters can also make the exact opposite choices and end up rolling completely different Pools for the same Skill. Either way, this axis of your character stays put, giving you something dependable to build the rest of your identity around. either mechanically or naratively.

## Favored Skills

Once you've assigned all nine Skills to a Pool and chosen your Practitioner Type ([[2. Practitioners - Cast, Wield, Shape]]), pick **2 of your 9 Skills to Favor.**

This is deliberately the last step when choosing where you skills sit under each Blood, Might or Verve pool. By the time you pick, you already know which of your Skills landed in a Pool that won't see as much use compared to your Practitioner Type's stronger Pool. Favoring a Skill in that weaker Pool gives it teeth when rolled, even though the Pool it rolls from is a smaller part of your build. Favoring a Skill in your *dominant* Pool instead just makes you even more dangerous in the area you already lean into.

**Mechanical effect:** whenever you roll a check using a Favored Skill, you may **spend 1 Pip** (from the Pool the skill belongs to) to add your current **Favored Skill Bonus** directly to the roll's total. That bonus grows with Level: see the Level-Up Baseline ([[4. Experiences & Levelling Up]]) for the current numbers. Both Favored Skills share that same single bonus number and the same 1-Pip cost.

Note: The bonus received cannot stack with combat bonus. Mechanically skill benefits should only ever give benefits to skill checks rather than combat roles, and vice versa.
