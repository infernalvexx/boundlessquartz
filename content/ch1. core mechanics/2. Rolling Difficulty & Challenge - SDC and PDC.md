# Rolling Die, Difficulty & Challenge: SDC / PDC

Two difficulty numbers govern the contentious moment in the game, and they're built to look alike on purpose, because they work identically, only the direction of the check changes, ie player facing or scene/GM facing.

> **Scene Difficulty Challenge (SDC)**
> How hard *the world* is to beat. Your GM sets this whenever you act against something: picking a lock, striking a foe, talking your way past a guard.

> **Player Difficulty Challenge (PDC)**
> How hard *you* are to beat. The mirror image of SDC, rolled against whenever something tries to strike or affect you.

## Outcome Bands
Every check resolves into one of three outcomes, set by two thresholds - a **Low Bar** and a **High Bar**:

| Bar                | Result                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Below the Low Bar  | **Miss or failure**                                                                                                          |
| Above the Low Bar  | **Blocked** *(or Dodged / Partial, depending on the fiction)*, partial success or partial failure depending on the narrative |
| Above the High Bar | **A clean Hit** or a solid success                                                                                           |

| Outlier     | Result                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 3 of a kind | Regardless of which numbers a rolled, 3 of a kind is a rare and allows you to add whatever flavor you wish to the resulting action taken after this beautiful success |

A default gap between the two bars exists- the **Margin**- as a flat 3 when you create your character and begin the game at level 1. As you expand your characters abilities and further customise how your character grows this middle bar can be made slim, wide or maintain the 3 number Margin (See Ch2. p2 for a deeper breakdown of level-based margin alterations).

> *Say an enemy's SDC has a Low Bar of 8 and a High Bar of 11. Rolling a 7 misses entirely; rolling a 9 lands a Blocked  pr partially successful result; rolling a 12 is a clean Hit.*

## Setting Scene Difficulty Challenge (SDC)

Rather than a GM guessing at a fair number, **SDC is derived mathematically from the party's Expected Rolls** (Ch1 §1). This keeps difficulty consistent from scene to scene, and it recalibrates automatically as the party grows - nobody has to wonder whether a fight was tuned for a different table. 

> Specific enemy or environment archetypes can layer a flat +x Low Bar, and +y High Bar on top of that baseline

The GM can build enemies to suit the strengths and weaknesses of their party by pulling this low and high bar lever, making one enemy type harder to land a hit on and a different one harder to hit outright, without rebuilding the whole difficulty system per monster.

In addition to the enemy specific difficulty changes the GM can also bump up the general Scenes DC based on whether or not the narrative calls for it- this naturally encourages players to play more flippantly with a simpler scene, like a bar brawl, compared to a series scene like the final showdown in the castles throne room. The narrative gravity naturally increases difficulty or expectation of the party to plan and think critically before engaging whatever is laid out in front of them.

## Setting Player Difficulty Challenge (PDC)

Your own PDC's baseline Low/High Bar uses a formula of its own:

> **PDC Low Bar = 4 + (Level ÷ 2, rounded up) + Practitioner Bonus.** **PDC High Bar = Low Bar + 3.**

**Practitioner Bonus** is the PDC Low/High bar bonus looked up from your Practitioner Type's own table (See Ch2. p2 for more information on Practitioners) at your current Level. On top of that core result, both the shared Level-Up Baseline and your Practitioner Type's table (See Ch2. p1 for baseline level up content) add a further PDC bonus as you level, stacking additively rather than replacing the baseline formula.
