# Rolling Difficulty & Challenge: SDC / PDC

Two difficulty numbers govern every contested moment in the game, and they're built to look alike on purpose, because they work identically, only the direction of the check changes.

- **Scene Difficulty Class (SDC)** - how hard *the world* is to beat. Your GM sets this whenever you act against something: picking a lock, striking a foe, talking your way past a guard.
- **Player Difficulty Class (PDC)** - how hard *you* are to beat. The mirror image of SDC, rolled against whenever something tries to strike or affect you.

## Outcome Bands

Every check resolves into one of three outcomes, set by two thresholds - a **Low Bar** and a **High Bar**:

| Result | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Miss** | Roll falls below the Low Bar |
| **Blocked** *(or Dodged / Partial, depending on the fiction)* | Roll lands between the Low Bar and the High Bar |
| **Hit** | Roll meets or beats the High Bar |

The default gap between the two bars- the **Margin**- is a flat 3. Enemies, environments, and defensive builds can widen or narrow that gap, but 3 is the average/lower difficulty that is chosen when a scene is set.

*Say an enemy's SDC has a Low Bar of 8 and a High Bar of 11. Rolling a 7 misses entirely; rolling a 9 lands a Blocked  pr partially successful result; rolling a 12 is a clean Hit.*

## Setting SDC

Rather than a GM guessing at a fair number, **SDC is derived mathematically from the party's Expected Rolls** (Ch1 §1). This keeps difficulty consistent from scene to scene, and it recalibrates automatically as the party grows - nobody has to wonder whether a fight was tuned for a different table. Specific enemy or environment archetypes can layer a flat `+x Low Bar, +y High Bar` on top of that baseline, making one enemy type harder to land a hit on and a different one harder to hit outright, without rebuilding the whole difficulty system per monster.

## Setting PDC

Your own PDC's baseline Low/High Bar pair uses a formula of its own:

> **PDC Low Bar = 4 + (Level ÷ 2, rounded up) + Practitioner Bonus.** **PDC High Bar = Low Bar + 3.**

**Practitioner Bonus** is the PDC Low/High bar bonus looked up from your Practitioner Type's own table (Ch2 §2) at your current Level. On top of that core result, both the shared Level-Up Baseline and your Practitioner Type's table (Ch2 §1; Ch2 §2) add a further PDC bonus as you level, stacking additively rather than replacing the formula above — the same layering pattern Speed uses (Ch3 §4).

Your defense lives on the exact same three-band Miss/Blocked/Hit logic as everything you do to the world, rather than being an isolated "armor class" number that plays by different rules.
