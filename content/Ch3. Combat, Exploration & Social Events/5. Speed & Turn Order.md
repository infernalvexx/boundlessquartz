# Turn Order
Turn order runs on **Speed**: a standing number on your character sheet, always known and never rolled for on the fly.

## Speed Is a Fact, Not a Roll
Outside combat, your GM can use your Speed as a passive lever for exploration and social scenes without you rolling it, though they may ask for it to raise tension or give clarity to you. A die roll only enters the picture once per encounter, at the top of combat, to set that fight's order. A pool is chosen based on your practitioner type, and your speed stat is added to that roll to set the initial turns orner.

**Baseline Speed** comes from three inputs at character creation: your Level ([Ch2. p6](obsidian://open?vault=TTRPG&file=Master%2F1.%20Version%202%2FCh2.%20Character%20Creation%20%26%20Development%2F6.%20Power%20Scale)), your bonded element's Reality Behavior (a fast-reading element sets a higher baseline than a slow, inert one [Ch4. p5](obsidian://open?vault=TTRPG&file=Master%2F1.%20Version%202%2FCh4.%20The%20Elements%20of%20the%20World%2F5.%20Natural%2C%20Bound%20%26%20Planar%20Elements)), and your Practitioner Type ([Ch2. p2](obsidian://open?vault=TTRPG&file=Master%2F1.%20Version%202%2FCh2.%20Character%20Creation%20%26%20Development%2F2.%20Practitioners%20-%20Cast%2C%20Wield%2C%20Shape)). Casters and Wielders lean their respective Pool one way or another, while Shapers carry the lowest baseline of the three in exchange for extra flexibility.

**This formula isn't replaced as you level.** Both the shared Level-Up Baseline ([Ch2. p1](obsidian://open?vault=TTRPG&file=Master%2F1.%20Version%202%2FCh2.%20Character%20Creation%20%26%20Development%2F1.%20Character%20Sheet)) and your Practitioner Type's own table ([Ch2. p2](obsidian://open?vault=TTRPG&file=Master%2F1.%20Version%202%2FCh2.%20Character%20Creation%20%26%20Development%2F2.%20Practitioners%20-%20Cast%2C%20Wield%2C%20Shape)) contribute a further Speed bonus at each level, stacking on top of the Level term above rather than overriding it.

## Round Structure
Combat runs on a single turn queue. One character acts at a time, in Speed order, high to low. A round ends once everyone has acted. The queue locks at the top of the round: anything you do this turn can only affect where you sit in *next* round's queue, never this one.
## Encounter Speed Shifts
Every combat round after the first, when every character in the scene/encounter acts, the GM monitors the tally of +/neutral/− alterations carried by each Skill assigned to whichever Pool you rolled.
Every action feeds two numbers into your Speed for the following round added together: the value carried by its Ability Type and the value carried by the Approach you chose to color it with.

| Type     | Speed Value |
| -------- | ----------- |
| Harmony  | 4           |
| Sense    | 3           |
| Burst    | 2           |
| Creation | 1           |
 **Per action: Speed swing = Ability Type value + Approach value.**

>*Ren Talbot lands an Earth Spike (Burst, value 2) delivered with Nerve (his favored Approach, value 4) — a swing of 6 for next round. The same Burst delivered with Calculation (1) would give only 3: the strike lands identically either way, but how Ren chose to make it costs real tempo.*

Because you take **2 actions per turn** ([Ch3. p5](obsidian://open?vault=TTRPG&file=Master%2F1.%20Version%202%2FCh3.%20Combat%2C%20Exploration%20%26%20Social%20Events%2F5.%20Action%20Economy)), this speed alteration happens twice, and both totals stack into your next-round Speed. Turn order is meant to keep shifting as a direct consequence of what you chose to do and not settle into a static line-up.
## Ties
Tied characters each roll their highest Core Resource Pool (a normal 3-die sum. Higher roll wins and acts first.
## Speed-for-Action Trade
If your Speed sits 10 or more above the highest Speed in the round, you may spend 10 Speed to take an extra action that turn. The 10 spent feeds directly into your next-round Speed swing above, rather than being tracked separately.
