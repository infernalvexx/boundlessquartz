# Action Economy

## Actions Per Turn

You get a flat **2 actions**, every round, regardless of your Speed. There's no scaling by Speed tier — Speed's payoff is entirely about *where you sit in the queue and how much you can react*, not a bigger action budget.

**Free movement** is baseline and revocable — hostile abilities or difficult terrain can strip it, forcing movement to come out of your 2-action budget instead.

## Reactions & Tandem Actions

Whether you have a reaction available at all this round depends on clearing a flat Speed threshold. If your Speed sits above the round's average, you get **+1 additional reaction or tandem action** on top of that.

**Tandem Actions** spend a reaction, not a full action. The acting character pays a normal action; the assisting character pays their reaction. The assist resolves as a **Swap-in**: the assisting character's own relevant die replaces the actor's lowest die in the 3-die pool, if it's higher.

*Kess swings and rolls a 2, a 3, and a 1 — a 6. Dell, reacting alongside them, rolls a single d6 and gets a 5. Since 5 beats Kess's lowest die (the 1), it swaps in: the roll becomes 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 instead of 6, still a clean 3-die sum, just with one die borrowed from an ally.*

Being fast buys you more chances to intervene in others' turns, not a bigger turn of your own — the faster you are, the more impact you can have on a fight beyond just your own actions.

## Interaction With Dice-Stepping and Pips

Whether stepping a die should ever cost an action, unlock an overflow action, or help you cross the reaction threshold is still undecided. Assume for now that stepping is purely a Pip cost with no action-economy interaction, until your table hears otherwise.
