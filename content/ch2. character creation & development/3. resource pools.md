# Resource Pools

Everything you do at the table draws from three Core Resource Pools.

| Pool | Governs |
|---|---|
| **Blood** | Health, survival, injury resistance |
| **Might** | Physical strength and willpower |
| **Verve** | Attunement, sensitivity, arcane capability |

## Two Layers, Per Pool

Each Pool carries two separate layers:

1. **The 3-die slot pool** — your three dice (Ch1 §1). This grows only through leveling and the choices you make building your Legacy; you don't spend it moment to moment.
2. **Pips** — a separate, spendable currency layered on top, with its own independent refresh cycle. Pips are what you burn turn to turn to step a die, fuel an ability, or trigger an Approach (Ch3 §3).

## Resource Protection

By default, **Blood is protected**: before Blood damage lands, you or your GM can choose to spend Might or Verve Pips, one for one, to prevent it. Might and Verve take the hit so your Blood doesn't have to.

That's the default, not a fixed law. Some Legacies flip which resource is protected, spending Blood freely and directly instead, in exchange for a different tradeoff elsewhere in that character's kit. If your Legacy or Identity inverts Resource Protection, note it clearly on your sheet, since it changes how everyone at the table should expect your Pools to behave.

Because Blood is protected by default, running low on Might or Verve tends to read as "spent, drained, out of tricks," while losing Blood reads as genuine physical danger. A character who deliberately inverts that protection is making a statement about who they are — spending their own vitality on purpose is the point.

## Die-Stepping

Your dice grow in two different ways.

**Permanent die-stepping.** How many die-steps you have available by a given Level is no longer a flat 1-per-level — it follows the Level-Up Baseline (Ch2 §1), which front-loads unevenly rather than handing out one step every single level (nothing at Level 1, two at once at Level 5, for example). The Pip cost per step is unchanged regardless of when the step arrives: d4→d6 costs 1 Pip, d6→d8 costs 2, d8→d10 costs 3.

*Stepping a d4 up to a d6 costs a single Pip — cheap, since it's the smallest jump. Taking that same die from d8 to d10 costs 3, reflecting how much more that die is already carrying.*

**Temporary die-stepping.** Scene-scale stepping — not permanent — scales the same direction as permanent stepping, but cheaper: 1, 1, and 2 Pips across the three step tiers. These numbers are a working default rather than a fully settled one, since play revealed characters step dice more constantly than first assumed.

## Pool Pip Bonus

Beyond die-stepping, your Pip capacity in each Pool grows on its own separate track. Both the shared Level-Up Baseline and your Practitioner Type's own table (Ch2 §2) contribute a **Pool Pip Bonus** at each level, and the two stack together — a Caster's own table front-loads this early, a Wielder's later, each type pacing it differently. Add both numbers to your running Pip Cap on top of whatever your die-tier growth already provides.
